# App Review checklist

Each numbered item: **pass** (proof), **fail** (file:line and the guideline's words), **can't tell from the code** (what would settle it), or N/A (why). ★ = a common first-submission rejection.

Checked against Apple's own documents on 16 Nov 2026 (App Review Guidelines "Last Updated: October 16, 2026"; Developer Program License Agreement LYL262, October 29, 2026). The exact quotes, pages and dates behind every row are in `apple-docs.md`. **Re-verify every number and rule against the current page before a run**: developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines, the Upcoming Requirements page (developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements), and for Google, the Play Developer Policy Center. Write the date in the report.

## 0. Get the real build and metadata

```bash
# The archive (Xcode Organizer › Show in Finder) or an exported .ipa
unzip -q App.ipa -d ipa && APP=$(ls -d ipa/Payload/*.app); BIN="$APP/$(basename "$APP" .app)"
plutil -p "$APP/Info.plist" | grep -E "UsageDescription|CFBundle(ShortVersion|Version)|UIBackgroundModes|ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption|UILaunch|MinimumOSVersion|DTSDKName|DTXcode|UIDeviceFamily|UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities"
codesign -d --entitlements :- "$APP"                                  # app entitlements
for x in "$APP"/PlugIns/*.appex; do echo "$x"; codesign -d --entitlements :- "$x"; done   # every extension
find "$APP" -name PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy -print -exec plutil -lint {} \;   # app + SDK privacy manifests, and that each parses
strings "$BIN" | grep -iE "lorem|placeholder|coming soon|beta|todo|localhost|staging|test_[a-z0-9]" | sort -u
for b in "$BIN" "$APP"/Frameworks/*.framework; do [ -d "$b" ] && b="$b/$(basename "$b" .framework)"; nm -u "$b" 2>/dev/null | grep -qE "_(f?stat|lstat|fstatat|f?statfs|f?statvfs|f?getattrlist|getattrlistbulk|getattrlistat|mach_absolute_time)$" && echo "required-reason C API in $b"; done
grep -rnE "UserDefaults|creationDate|modificationDate|contentModificationDateKey|creationDateKey|systemUptime|volume(Available|Total)Capacity|systemFreeSize|systemSize|activeInputModes" --include=*.swift --include=*.m .   # required-reason APIs in source
grep -rnE "([0-9]{1,3}\.){3}[0-9]{1,3}|inet_addr|gethostbyname|sockaddr_in[^6]|AF_INET[^6]" --include=*.swift --include=*.m --include=*.c .   # IPv4-only code (2.5.5)
```
Xcode: Organizer › right-click the archive › **Generate Privacy Report** (all manifests merged). Links: `curl -sIL <url> | grep -E "^HTTP"` for every URL in the app and the listing. Metadata: the App Store Connect API (read-only) or the owner's screenshots of each App Store Connect page: App Information, Pricing and Availability, App Privacy, Monetization › Subscriptions, the version page, App Review, Business › Agreements.

Walk it like a reviewer: fresh install, signed out, no purchases, on the latest released iOS ("Your app will be tested on a device running the latest released version of iOS", developer.apple.com/support/ipv6), on an iPhone **and an iPad** (iPhone-only apps still run on iPad in compatibility mode and reviewers use iPads), once on an IPv6-only network.

## 1. Completeness and the build (2.1, 2.2, 2.3.1, 2.5)

1. ★ **Launches and runs the main flows without a crash on a fresh install, on iPhone and iPad (2.1).** 2.1(a): "We will reject incomplete app bundles and binaries that crash or exhibit obvious technical problems"; "apps that crash on launch will be removed immediately". Check: delete the app, install the Release build (TestFlight or the archive) on a real iPhone and an iPad (or iPad simulator) on the latest released iOS, signed out; walk launch, onboarding, sign-in, paywall, purchase, the main feature, settings, deletion; read Xcode Organizer › Crashes and the crash reporter for that build.
2. ★ **No placeholder content, lorem ipsum, empty screens, "coming soon" or features that do nothing (2.1).** 2.1(a): "placeholder text, empty websites, and other temporary content should be scrubbed before submission." Check: the `strings` scan in §0; read every screen's copy; every list fed by live data has a real empty state.
3. ★ **Backend services are live and the demo account works for the whole review window (2.1).** "Enable backend services so that they’re live and accessible during review"; the demo account "must not expire". Check: from a clean device on cellular, sign in with the demo account; no IP, country or allow-list blocks on the API, auth or AI provider; rate limits won't lock a reviewer out.
4. ★ **In-app purchases are complete and submitted together (2.1(b)).** "make sure they are complete, up-to-date, visible to the reviewer and functional." The first subscription "must be submitted with a new app version", "together with its subscription group", all "added to the same draft submission". Required per product: Display Name, Description, **App Review Screenshot** (review notes are optional, not required). Check: App Store Connect › Monetization › Subscriptions: every product "Ready to Submit", localized, priced in every storefront, screenshot uploaded; App Review › Drafts holds the version, the group and every product.
5. ★ **No beta, trial-version or test wording, no debug menus, test bars, fixture data or free pass in the Release build (2.2).** "Demos, betas, and trial versions of your app don’t belong on the App Store – use TestFlight instead." Check: the `strings` scan of the archive, not the source; Release build flags; TestFlight and the App Store get the same binary, so test switches must check at runtime.
6. **Every link in the app works: privacy, terms, support, help, external pages (2.1).** "All links in your app must be functional. A link to user support with up-to-date contact information and a link to your privacy policy is required for all apps." Check: `strings "$BIN" | grep -oE "https?://[^\"' ]+" | sort -u`, then `curl -sIL` each; open each on the phone.
7. **Built with the toolchain Apple requires (Upcoming Requirements).** "must be built with Xcode 26 or later using an SDK for iOS 26" (since 28 Apr 2026); "must target iOS 13 or later" (since 9 Sep 2026). Check: `DTXcode` 2600 or higher, `DTSDKName` iphoneos26 or later, `MinimumOSVersion` 13.0 or higher in the archive's Info.plist; re-read the page for newer entries.
8. **Only public APIs (2.5.1); background modes only for their purpose (2.5.4).** "Apps may only use public APIs"; background services "only ... for their intended purposes". Check: each `UIBackgroundModes` value has a feature that uses it (audio really plays, location really tracks); no private selectors in `strings`.
9. **No hidden, dormant or remotely switched features that change what the reviewer saw (2.5.19, 2.3.1(a)).** 2.5.19: "Don’t include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features"; apps get removed when "modified after approval, such as through a server-side change, in a way that introduces a violation". 2.5.19 is new on 16 Oct 2026 (it was part of 2.3.1); Apple's note on it: "adding features or functionality that weren’t included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review" leads to "removal from the App Store and removal from the Apple Developer Program." Check: list every remote flag and config key, its value during review and who can change it; describe the ones that matter in the review notes.
10. **Push notifications are not required and not used for marketing without opt-in (4.5.4).** "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function"; marketing only with "consent language displayed in your app’s UI" and an in-app opt-out. Check: decline at the system prompt and keep using the app; find the marketing-push consent and its off switch.
11. **Fully functional on an IPv6-only network (2.5.5).** "Apps must be fully functional on IPv6-only networks." Check: Mac on Ethernet, Option-click Sharing › Internet Sharing › "Create NAT64 Network", share over Wi-Fi; the phone joins with cellular off; run sign-in, purchase, sync, AI and every network call; the §0 grep for IPv4 literals and IPv4-only APIs, including SDK endpoints and any embedded server.
12. **A launch screen key when built with the iOS 27 SDK (TN3208).** App Store Connect "validates that your app’s `Info.plist` contains at least one of" `UILaunchStoryboardName`, `UILaunchStoryboards`, `UILaunchScreen`, `UILaunchScreens`, or rejects the upload with "ITMS-90870: Missing launch screen." Check: the §0 `plutil` line shows one of them.
13. **Export compliance answered (encryption).** Without `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` the questionnaire comes back on every upload, and a build in "Missing Compliance" can't be submitted. OS encryption such as HTTPS through URLSession "is exempt from export documentation upload requirements"; proprietary or non-OS algorithms need documents (French declaration, CCATS). Check: the key in Info.plist (`NO` when only OS encryption is used); App Store Connect › the build's compliance status; App Information › App Encryption Documentation when required. Note for the owner: exempt apps may still owe the U.S. annual self-classification report.

## 2. Purchases and subscriptions (3.1.1, 3.1.2, 3.1.3, Schedule 2)

14. ★ **Digital features and content are unlocked only through in-app purchase (3.1.1).** "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app ... you must use in-app purchase." "Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys". Check: grep for web checkouts (Stripe, Paddle, PayPal links), unlock codes, QR or licence keys; every paid gate reads a StoreKit (or billing SDK) entitlement.
15. **No buttons, links or calls to action to other purchase methods, except where allowed (3.1.1(a), 3.1.3).** Verified 16 Nov 2026: the entitlements "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps"; elsewhere without an entitlement apps "may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase". Check: "cheaper on our website" copy or pricing links in the app and the listing; if shown in the US only, the storefront check (`Storefront.current?.countryCode`) gates it.
16. ★ **Before subscribing, the screen says what they get for the price (3.1.2(c)).** "clearly describe what the user will get for the price. How many issues per month? How much cloud storage? What kind of access to your service?" Check: the paywall names concrete benefits and limits (how many exports, how much storage, which features), the same limits the server enforces.
17. **The title of each subscription is shown (Schedule 2 §3.8(b)).** "Title of auto-renewing subscription, which may be the same as the in-app product name". Check: each plan shows its name, matching the Subscription Display Name in App Store Connect (`Product.displayName`).
18. **The length of each subscription is shown (Schedule 2 §3.8(b)).** "Length of subscription"; the sign-up screen must include "Subscription name and duration, and the content or services provided during the subscription period". Check: "1 year", "1 month" next to each price, from `product.subscription?.subscriptionPeriod`.
19. ★ **The price of each subscription comes from the store, in the buyer's currency (Schedule 2 §3.8(b), subscriptions page).** "Price of subscription, and price per unit if appropriate"; "Full renewal price, shown clearly and prominently, and localized in available currencies"; "Fetch product information using StoreKit APIs before presenting In-App Purchases in your app's UI". Check: prices render from `Product.displayPrice` (or the billing SDK's localized string), never a hard-coded "$"; change the sandbox tester's storefront (App Store Connect › Users and Access › Sandbox) and the currency follows.
20. ★ **The amount billed is the most prominent price (subscriptions page).** "the amount that will be billed must be the most prominent pricing element in the layout"; per-week or per-month breakdowns "in a subordinate position and size". Check: on the yearly plan the yearly total is bigger or bolder than any breakdown; screenshot on the smallest iPhone and on iPad.
21. ★ **A free trial says how long it is and what is charged after (subscriptions page, HIG).** "clearly indicate how long the free trial lasts and the price billed once the free trial is over"; people must know "a payment will be automatically initiated for the next subscription period." Check: next to the button, the trial length and the price after it ("3 days free, then <price> a year"), built from `introductoryOffer` (`period`, `paymentMode == .freeTrial`) and `displayPrice`.
22. **An introductory price states the offer price, its length and the standard price after (HIG).** "list the introductory price, the duration of the offer, and the standard price the customer pays after the offer ends." Check: every pay-as-you-go or pay-up-front offer, exit offers included, shows all three from the offer's `displayPrice`, `period`, `periodCount` and the product's `displayPrice`.
23. **Auto-renewal and how to cancel are stated by the button (Schedule 2 §3.10, HIG, renewal laws).** Schedule 2 §3.8(b) no longer lists renewal wording, but §3.10 requires selling subscriptions "in compliance with all legal and regulatory requirements" and auto-renewal laws want it by the button. Check: a line such as "Renews automatically at <price> a <period> until cancelled. Cancel anytime in Settings." on every purchase screen. Renewal law in depth: `ungenerated-audit-privacy`.
24. ★ **Terms of Use (EULA) and Privacy Policy links, in the app and in the metadata (Schedule 2 §3.8(b), subscriptions page, 5.1.1(i)).** "Links to Your Privacy Policy and Terms of Use must be accessible within Your Licensed Application"; "your app and App Store metadata must include links to your Terms of Use and Privacy Policy." With Apple's standard EULA "the license agreement link isn’t shown on your App Store product page", so the description carries the terms link (your terms, or https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/appstore/dev/stdeula/). Check: tap both links on every purchase screen (paywall, offers, settings); `curl -sIL` both; the description's last lines; App Privacy › Privacy Policy URL; App Information › License Agreement.
25. ★ **Restore Purchases is visible on the paywall and in settings (3.1.1, subscriptions page).** The sign-up screen must include "A way for current subscribers to sign in or restore purchases"; 3.1.1: "make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." Check: the button calls `AppStore.sync()` (or the SDK's restore); buy in the sandbox, delete the app, reinstall, Restore unlocks; with nothing to restore, a clear message.
26. **Subscriptions give ongoing value, last at least seven days and work on all the person's devices (3.1.2(a)).** "the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user’s devices." Check: durations in App Store Connect (weekly or longer, Schedule 2 §3.8(a)); the entitlement shows on a second device with the same Apple Account and after sign-in on iPad.
27. ★ **A trial or intro price is promised only to accounts eligible for it; ineligible accounts see Subscribe copy.** "use receipt validation to ensure that you’re only showing offers to people who can redeem them"; "New and returning customers are only eligible to use one introductory offer per subscription group." `isEligibleForIntroOffer` "may be `true` even if you haven’t set up an introductory offer". Check: a sandbox tester who used the trial (clear purchase history to reset) sees Subscribe copy and no trial steps; the code checks eligibility and the offer's existence.
28. **Every discount shown is a real store offer of the right kind (2.3.1, 3.1.2(a)).** Introductory (new to the group), promotional (existing or lapsed subscribers, signed with the In-App Purchase key), win-back ("Your subscription needs to be approved by App Review before you can create a win-back offer", so none in a first submission), offer codes. 3.1.2(a): "bait-and-switch and scam practices ... will be removed". Check: each discounted screen maps to an offer in App Store Connect, the purchase call passes that offer, and nothing falls back to the full-price product.
29. **The paywall can be closed, or the hard paywall is clear about what is free and what isn't; nothing tricks the person into buying (3.1.2, 5.6).** 5.6: apps should never "trick them into making unwanted purchases". Check: a visible close; no countdown that resets; no pre-selected costlier plan presented as the only one.
30. ★ **Managing and cancelling use Apple's own sheet (subscriptions page, HIG).** `showManageSubscriptions(in:)` / `manageSubscriptionsSheet(isPresented:)`; "always make it easy for people to access the system-provided management UI where they can cancel if they wish"; on Mac "if isiOSAppOnMac is `true,` avoid showing the user interface for this feature." Check: Settings › Subscription opens Apple's sheet in one tap; the state read back afterwards (`renewalInfo.willAutoRenew` or the server), never a local "cancelled" flag.
31. **Sign-in before purchase only when the subscription has account-based features, and the notes say why (5.1.1(v)).** "If your app doesn’t include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." Check: if the paywall needs an account, the reason (sync across devices, the web) is real and written in the review notes.
32. **The Paid Apps Agreement, bank and tax are Active (configure in-app purchases for review).** "Paid In-App Purchases won't function in review until this agreement is active." Check: App Store Connect › Business › Agreements: Paid Apps "Active"; Bank Accounts and Tax Forms "Active" (TN3186). The API can't read it; ask the owner for a screenshot.
33. **Apple's purchase confirmation sheet is used as it is (HIG).** "Use the default confirmation sheet. ... Don’t modify or replicate this sheet." Check: the buy button calls `product.purchase()` (or the SDK's purchase) directly; no custom "Confirm purchase" sheet imitating Apple's; nothing drawn over it.
34. **One subscription group with ranked levels; no way to subscribe twice (3.1.2(b)).** "should not be able to inadvertently subscribe to multiple variations of the same thing"; HIG: "Encourage a new subscription only when someone isn’t already a subscriber." Check: App Store Connect › Subscriptions: plans in one group, levels ranked; a subscriber opening the paywall sees their plan or Manage, not Subscribe.
35. **Purchase help opens Apple's refund flow with nothing in front of it (HIG, StoreKit).** "Make it easy for people to request a refund"; "Avoid characterizing or providing guidance on Apple’s refund policies." Check: if the app has purchase help, Refund calls `beginRefundRequest(in:)` / `refundRequestSheet`, is visible without scrolling and promises no outcome. N/A if the app has no purchase help.
36. **Multiseat purchases are decided, not left at the default (App Store Connect).** "Multiseat purchasing is enabled by default in App Store Connect, unless you’ve previously adjusted this setting"; only subscriptions in apps without StoreKit 2, or with Family Sharing on, start opted out. Apple, 16 Sep 2026: "Starting today, multiseat purchases are enabled by default for subscriptions"; Volume Purchasing launched 22 Oct 2026, Group Purchases "this winter". Check: App Store Connect › Subscriptions › each subscription › Purchase Options; if allowed, the app grants and revokes group members' access (`SUBSCRIBED`, `REVOKE`, `REFUND` with subtype `SEATS`); if not, it's turned off.

## 3. Accounts and sign-in (5.1.1(v), 4.8)

37. ★ **An app that lets people create an account lets them delete it inside the app: easy to find, the whole account (5.1.1(v)).** "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." "Offer to delete the entire account record ... only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient." No "phone call, send an email, use a chat support flow"; confirmation steps are fine, but "apps that make it unnecessarily difficult for someone to delete their account will not pass review." Guest accounts and shared user content count; it works in every country. Check: Settings › Account › Delete in a few taps; afterwards the server rows are gone (query) and signing in again creates a new, empty account.
38. ★ **Deletion says the App Store subscription is not cancelled by deleting, and links to managing it (5.1.1(v) help).** "notify them that billing will continue through Apple and ask them to cancel their subscription before continuing"; `showManageSubscriptions` or https://apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions/; refunds through `beginRefundRequest` or https://support.apple.com/118223; a scheduled deletion only "as long as there's also an option to delete the account immediately." Check: start deletion as a sandbox subscriber: the screen says it plainly with a Manage button.
39. **Deletion says how long it takes and confirms when it's done (5.1.1(v) help, HIG).** "Inform the person how long it will take to delete the account and provide a confirmation when the deletion is complete." HIG: the link is not buried "in your Privacy Policy or Terms of Service pages", and app and web flows are equally easy. Check: the screen after deleting; a website deletion page, if any, is no harder than the app.
40. ★ **If a third-party or social login is the primary login, an equivalent login option is offered (4.8).** It must limit data "to the user’s name and email address", let people "keep their email address private", and not collect interactions "for advertising purposes without consent." 4.8 doesn't name Sign in with Apple; it meets all three. Exceptions: own sign-in only, education or enterprise accounts, citizen ID, a client for one third-party service. Check: the sign-in screen's options.
41. **Sign in with Apple tokens are revoked through Apple's REST API when the account is deleted (5.1.1(v) help, TN3194).** Verified: "Apps that support Sign in with Apple should use the Sign in with Apple REST API to revoke user tokens." That needs the refresh token stored at sign-up (validate the authorization code with `/auth/token`), then `POST https://appleid.apple.com/auth/revoke` with `client_id`, `client_secret`, `token`, `token_type_hint`; also handle `credentialRevokedNotification` and the server events `consent-revoked` and `account-deleted`. Check: grep the server for `auth/token` and `auth/revoke`; delete a test account and sign in again: Apple shows the first-time name and email sheet (if it doesn't, the token wasn't revoked, TN3194).
42. **The app doesn't require sign-in without significant account-based features, and asks only for what it needs (5.1.1(v), 5.1.1(x)).** "Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law"; name and email only "so long as the request is optional for the user". Check: can a new person reach the core feature, and is every sign-up field necessary?
43. **The Sign in with Apple button follows Apple's rules (HIG).** "no smaller than other sign-in buttons, and avoid making people scroll to see the button"; at least 140 x 30 pt; custom buttons only titled "Sign in with Apple", "Sign up with Apple" or "Continue with Apple", black or white, Apple's logo artwork; "App Review evaluates all custom Sign in with Apple buttons." No password and no personal-email request from people who chose the relay. Relay addresses are moving: new ones "will be issued on private.icloud.com", so "email validation logic, and allowlists" must accept it as well as privaterelay.appleid.com (Apple news, 24 Aug 2026). Check: the sign-in screen on the smallest iPhone; the button's code (`SignInWithAppleButton` or a custom one); grep the app and server for `privaterelay` and any email-domain allowlist.
44. **Social network connections can be revoked in the app; their tokens stay on the device (5.1.1(v)).** "The app must also include a mechanism to revoke social network credentials and disable data access between the app and social network from within the app. An app may not store credentials or tokens to social networks off of the device". Check: a Disconnect control for any connected social account; tokens in the Keychain only. N/A if the app connects to no social network.

## 4. Privacy (5.1.1, 5.1.2)

45. ★ **A privacy policy is linked in App Store Connect and inside the app, and says what Apple requires (5.1.1(i)).** "link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner"; it must "Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data", cover third parties' equal protection, and "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion". Check: App Privacy › Privacy Policy URL; the in-app link; the policy read against the data inventory (`ungenerated-audit-privacy`).
46. ★ **Every permission has a purpose string that says specifically why, localized, including SDKs' (5.1.1(ii)).** "A purpose string that only restates the name of the resource, or promises a general benefit without saying what your app actually does, doesn't meet the requirement." "Write one brief, complete sentence in active voice and sentence case, ending with a period." "you must provide localized purpose strings." Check: §0 `UsageDescription` keys, each `InfoPlist.strings`; every key has a feature using it, and APIs referenced without a reason are removed from the binary.
47. **Permissions are asked in context; any screen before a system prompt has one "Continue" button and no way out (5.1.1(iv), HIG).** "Apps must respect the user’s permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent"; HIG: "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert", "Use a term like “Continue” or “Next”", no "option to close or cancel". Declining keeps an alternative. Check: a fresh install, every prompt's timing, every pre-prompt screen's buttons, then decline each and keep using the app.
48. ★ **`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` is present and valid for the app and every SDK on Apple's list; every required-reason API is declared with an allowed reason.** Since 1 May 2024 "apps that don’t describe their use of required reason API in their privacy manifest file aren’t accepted"; "App Store Connect rejects app submissions that include invalid privacy manifest files"; since 12 Feb 2025 listed SDKs need their own valid manifest. Check: §0 `find`, `nm` and `grep` lines; each category used has a code from `apple-docs.md` §6; SDKs on the list in `ungenerated-audit-privacy/references/apple-privacy.md` carry their own manifest; Xcode's privacy report.
49. **App Store privacy labels match what the code and SDKs actually collect (labels page).** "even if you collect the data for reasons other than analytics or advertising, it still needs to be declared." Check: the inventory from `ungenerated-audit-privacy` against App Store Connect › App Privacy, type by type (free text is "Other User Content", voice "Audio Data", IP addresses by their use), with linked and tracking answered.
50. **App Tracking Transparency is asked only if the app tracks; no prompt and no IDFA otherwise (5.1.2(i)).** "You must receive explicit permission from users via the App Tracking Transparency APIs to track their activity." Check: `NSUserTrackingUsageDescription` and `ATTrackingManager` only with real tracking; `NSPrivacyTracking` false and no `NSPrivacyTrackingDomains` otherwise; SDKs that track.
51. ★ **Personal data sent to a third-party AI is clearly disclosed and the person gives explicit permission first (5.1.2(i)).** "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." Check: the app's own sheet before any text leaves the device, naming the provider and what is sent; decline and revoke paths (`ungenerated-audit-ai`).
52. **Data is used only for what it was collected for, and not to build profiles for unrelated uses (5.1.2(ii), (iii)).** "Data collected for one purpose may not be repurposed without further consent"; "Apps should not attempt to surreptitiously build a user profile". Check: analytics events and properties against the stated purposes.
53. **Collecting user or usage data has the person's consent, even when it's "anonymous" (5.1.1(ii)).** "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous"; "Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data"; "an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent." Check: what analytics and crash SDKs send before any choice; a consent step or a visible off switch in settings, stated in the policy. How App Review tests this isn't documented; report it as Apple's words, not as a certain rejection.
54. **No feature, content or reward depends on allowing push, location or tracking (5.1.2(i)).** "Your app may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation". Check: decline notifications in onboarding and continue; no reward for allowing.
55. **Recording asks first and shows that it's recording (2.5.14).** "Apps must request explicit user consent and provide a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording ... This includes any use of the device camera, microphone, screen recordings". Check: recording starts only on the person's action, with a visible indicator in the app as well as the system's.
56. **Pickers instead of full library access where possible (5.1.1(iii)).** "use the out-of-process picker or a share sheet rather than requesting full access to protected resources like Photos or Contacts." Check: photo and contact picking uses `PhotosPicker` / `PHPickerViewController` / `CNContactPickerViewController`; a full-access usage string exists only when the feature needs the whole library.

## 5. Metadata and store assets (2.3)

57. **Name, subtitle and keywords follow the limits and rules (2.3.7).** Name "at least two characters and no more than 30"; subtitle 30, and it must not "reference other apps, or make unverifiable product claims"; keywords "up to 100 bytes", no other app or company names, no repeats of the name; no prices or terms in names, subtitles, screenshots or previews. Check: App Store Connect › each localization; `printf '%s' "$KEYWORDS" | wc -c`.

Coming: iPhone Duo screenshots from April 2027; the specification page says "for any app using the iOS 27.1 SDK or later", Apple's 5 Oct 2026 news says "any apps or games submitted" (plan for the wider reading). Check: `sips -g pixelWidth -g pixelHeight -g hasAlpha *.png`; count app-in-use against marketing frames per device.
59. **Icon, screenshots, previews and creative assets suit a 4+ audience even if the app is rated higher (2.3.8, 2.3.14).** "adhere to a 4+ age rating even if your app is rated higher"; creative assets carry no "specific or discounted pricing information"; "For Kids" is reserved for the Kids Category. Check: view every asset as if for a child.
60. **The description lists only features this build has; no prices; paid parts are marked (2.3.1, 2.3.2).** 2.3.2: make clear "whether any featured items, levels, subscriptions, etc. require additional purchases"; Apple: "avoid including specific prices in your app description". Check: read the description against the build.
61. **The app preview uses footage captured from the app (2.3.4).** "previews may only use video screen captures of the app itself"; 15–30 seconds, up to 500 MB, 30 fps, iPhone 886 x 1920. Check: `ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration:stream=width,height,r_frame_rate preview.mp4`.
62. **The age rating questionnaire is answered truthfully for the 2025 system (2.3.6).** Answers have been required since 31 Jan 2026; the questions are in-app controls, capabilities (unrestricted web access, user-generated content, social media, messaging and chat, advertising), mature themes, medical or wellness, sexuality, violence, chance. There is no AI question as such: answer for what the AI lets people do. New from 16 Nov 2026 (Apple news, 2 Nov 2026): "Shared Location: Sharing of real-world location with another person or group" and "Dating and Relationships", which covers "a virtual companion that engages a user in sexual or romantic content" and gives "a minimum age rating of 18+"; required on any submission now, and "All apps must have these declarations completed by January 12, 2027." "If your app has a EULA with minimum age requirements that exceed the rating that Apple calculated, you must override to a rating that adheres to the requirements." Check: App Store Connect › App Information › Age Ratings against the build, and the terms' minimum age against the computed rating.
63. **The support URL works and leads to real contact details (1.5, App Store Connect).** "This URL must lead to actual contact information (legal address, email address, telephone number), as may be required by local law". Check: open it signed out: an email and a way to reach a person, matching the trader details if the app is sold in the EU as a trader.
64. **Apple's products and marks use Apple's official artwork, never an imitation or an emoji stand-in (5.2.5 and Apple's marketing guidelines).** Check: App Store badges, Apple Pay and Sign in with Apple marks come from Apple's resources.

Check: each page in App Store Connect; "Add for Review" stays disabled while any is missing.
66. **Content rights are declared truthfully and third-party content is licensed (App Store Connect, 5.2).** "Apps that contain, show, or access third-party content must have all the necessary rights to that content"; 5.2.2: "Authorization must be provided upon request." ## 6. Design and functionality (4.x)

72. ★ **The app does more than a website or a single screen: useful, unique, app-like (4.2).** "If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or “app-like,” it doesn’t belong on the App Store"; 4.2.2: not "web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links". Check: what is native and what is a web view; what still works offline.
73. **Not a copy of another app; the icon and name don't imitate another app or Apple (4.1, 5.2.5).** 4.1(c): "You cannot use another developer’s icon, brand, or product name in your app’s icon or name, without approval"; Apple's help: "Don't copy the look and feel of other apps." Check: name, icon and screens beside the category's top apps.
74. **If iPad is supported, layouts work on every iPad size and orientation the app claims.** Check: `UIDeviceFamily` and `UISupportedInterfaceOrientations~ipad`; the smallest and largest iPad simulators, both orientations, Split View and Stage Manager sizes.
75. **Every extension (share, widget, keyboard, intents) works, is signed with its entitlements, and is disclosed (4.4, 2.5.16).** 4.4: "clearly and accurately disclose what extensions are made available in the app’s marketing text, and the extensions may not include marketing, advertising, or in-app purchases." Check: §0 `codesign` per `.appex` (App Groups, push, Sign in with Apple); each extension used once; the description names it.
76. **Availability on Macs with Apple silicon and Apple Vision Pro is tested or turned off (App Store Connect).** iPhone and iPad apps "will be available to users on Apple Vision Pro unless you edit their availability"; the same for Macs. Check: App Store Connect › Pricing and Availability (Apple Silicon Mac Availability, Apple Vision Pro Availability); if on, run "Designed for iPad" on a Mac and the visionOS simulator through sign-in and purchase.
77. **Apple emoji appear only as text the system draws (4.5.6, 5.2.5).** "Apple emoji may not be used on other platforms or embedded directly in your app binary." Check: no emoji rendered into PNG or PDF assets (`find "$APP" -iname "*emoji*"` and a look through the asset catalogue); Unicode emoji in text are fine.
78. **Resources downloaded on first launch are disclosed first (4.2.3(ii)).** "disclose the size of the download and prompt users before doing so." Check: models, packs or media fetched before the app works show their size and ask.

## 9. The common rejections (check each even if the section passed)

Apple's own list (developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/, read 16 Nov 2026): crashes and bugs, broken links, placeholder content, incomplete information, privacy policy issues, unclear data access requests, inaccurate screenshots, substandard user interface, web clippings and link collections, repeated submission of similar apps, copycats, misleading users, not enough lasting value, submitted by the wrong entity. "On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1".

- Crash or bug on the reviewer's device, often iPad or the newest iOS (2.1).
- Demo account missing, expired, or unable to reach the paywall and buy (2.1).
- Paywall missing price per period, renewal wording, Terms or Privacy links (3.1.2).
- Terms of Use link missing from the description when the app uses Apple's standard EULA (3.1.2).
- No in-app account deletion (5.1.1(v)).
- Purpose strings too vague (5.1.1(ii)).
- A pre-permission screen with "Not now" or "Allow" buttons (5.1.1(iv), HIG).
- Screenshots that aren't the app or show features it doesn't have (2.3.3, 2.3.1).
- Placeholder content, beta wording, broken links (2.1, 2.2).
- Privacy labels that don't match the SDKs (5.1.2).
- Missing privacy manifest or required-reason declarations (rejected at upload or review).
- Sign-in required for no account-based reason (5.1.1(v)).
- Minimum functionality: a web wrapper or too thin (4.2).
- In-app purchases not submitted with the version (2.1(b)).
- A network call that fails on an IPv6-only network (2.5.5).

## 11. What developers who ship say gets rejected (Nov 2026)

From a developer's "approved in 48 hours" list, checked against Apple's text on 16 Nov 2026. Each is a row like the others.

87. ★ **No rating ask of your own, and none in onboarding.** Only `requestReview` / `AppStore.requestReview`, after a real success (a finished task), never during onboarding or the first session. A custom stars screen before the system sheet is a custom prompt: 5.6.1 "Use the provided API to prompt users to review your app ... and we will disallow custom review prompts." HIG: "Avoid asking for a rating on first launch or during onboarding"; StoreKit: "don’t call it in response to a button tap or other user action." Grep for star pickers, "rate us", "how's it going", `SKStoreReviewController`, `requestReview`, and where each is called.
88. ★ **No "coming soon", "soon", "not available yet", "later" labels, disabled tabs or empty sections** in any build a reviewer can reach, including empty states fed by live data (a category with no items yet). 2.1(a): "placeholder text, empty websites, and other temporary content should be scrubbed before submission." Grep the copy and check every live list for an empty state.
89. ★ **Managing or cancelling a subscription reaches Apple's own sheet without a gauntlet.** One tap from the subscription screen opens `showManageSubscriptions` / `manageSubscriptionsSheet`; a save offer or one question may sit beside it, never several screens in front of it, never a loop. HIG: "Always make it easy for customers to cancel an auto-renewable subscription." 5.6: "Apps should never prey on users ... trick them into making unwanted purchases ... or engage in any other manipulative practices within or outside of the app." Apple's own save step is the retention message it shows after "Cancel Subscription" (App Store Connect › Subscriptions › Retention Messaging).
90. **An offer after a declined purchase is shown once, closes, and is a real store offer** (an abandoned purchase sheet or a closed paywall): full terms like any purchase screen, no fake countdown, no paywall that reopens itself. 3.1.2(a): "Apps that attempt to scam users ... trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch ... will be removed." For lapsed subscribers Apple already shows its win-back sheet "within your app ... on iOS 18, iPadOS 18, and macOS 15 or later"; don't stack a second offer on it.
91. **Remote paywalls (Superwall, RevenueCat Paywalls, Adapty, your own JSON) pass items 16–25 in every live variant**, and no variant can be switched on after review that wouldn't. 2.3.1(a): "All new features, functionality, and product changes must be described with specificity in the Notes for Review". Apps are removed when "modified after approval, such as through a server-side change, in a way that introduces a violation." Say in the notes that the paywall is remote and which variant the reviewer sees.
92. **Privacy policy and terms are plain static pages**: HTML that loads without JavaScript, a login, a cookie wall or an embedded doc tool, at stable URLs linked from the app, the paywall, the listing and the website (5.1.1(i), 3.1.2(c) Schedule 2).
93. **The design is the app's own and doesn't look generated.** A 1:1 copy of another app's screens, name or icon gets a takedown (4.1, 5.2.5; Apple: "Don't copy the look and feel of other apps"); a template look with stock AI art and filler copy invites a closer read under 4.2 and 4.3(b) "indistinguishable from what's already widely available". Run `ungenerated-review` before submitting.
94. **Every screen works on iPad**, even for an iPhone-only app (it runs in compatibility mode and reviewers often use one): launch, onboarding, the paywall, purchase and the keyboard on an iPad simulator (2.1(a) "tested on-device for bugs and stability"). Apple: "Don't submit for review without testing your app's behavior in compatibility modes" and "Don't assume removing a supported destination in Xcode prevents distribution to that device type."
