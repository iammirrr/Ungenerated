What Apple's own pages require of an app on the App Store, by topic, in Apple's exact words (short quotes), with the page and the date it was read. In a report, cite the guideline or page, the quote and the date. Quotes are exact; "..." marks a cut. Payments go deeper in `ungenerated-audit-payments/references/apple-subscriptions.md`, privacy in `ungenerated-audit-privacy/references/apple-privacy.md`.

**All pages below were read on 16 Nov 2026.** Apple's own dates that day: App Review Guidelines "Last Updated: October 16, 2026"; Developer Program License Agreement version LYL262, "October 29, 2026"; HIG Apple In-App Purchase, change log "September 17, 2026"; newest Upcoming Requirements entry "Since September 9, 2026". Re-read a page when its date has moved.

What changed in the guidelines on 16 Oct 2026 (developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i, read 16 Nov 2026): 1.2 "Clarified app content and app types that are subject to this guideline"; 2.3.14 "New guideline covering creative assets"; 2.5.19 "New guideline clarifies long-standing policy on hidden, dormant, or undocumented features, formerly covered in Guideline 2.3.1"; 3.1.1(a) link entitlements merged; 5 child-exploitation removals. ## 1. Completeness and the build

**Apple's own count.** "On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness, which covers crashes, placeholder content, incomplete information, and more." (developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/, read 16 Nov 2026)

- **Final, tested, complete.** G 2.1(a): "Submissions to App Review ... should be final versions with all necessary metadata and fully functional URLs included; placeholder text, empty websites, and other temporary content should be scrubbed before submission." "Make sure your app has been tested on-device ... and include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login." "We will reject incomplete app bundles and binaries that crash or exhibit obvious technical problems."
- **Purchases complete and visible.** G 2.1(b): "If you offer in-app purchases in your app, make sure they are complete, up-to-date, visible to the reviewer and functional. If any configured in-app purchase items cannot be found or reviewed in your app, explain the reason in your review notes."
- **No betas.** G 2.2: "Demos, betas, and trial versions of your app don’t belong on the App Store – use TestFlight instead." TestFlight: "The first build you add to a group with external testers is sent to App Review" (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/test-compatible-devices, read 16 Nov 2026).
- **Links.** "All links in your app must be functional. A link to user support with up-to-date contact information and a link to your privacy policy is required for all apps." (developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Every device it can reach.** "Users expect the app to function on all the devices where it's available." "Apps designed for iPhone can run on iPad in compatibility mode if there are no dependencies on iPhone device capabilities." "Compatible iPhone and iPad apps can run unmodified on Macs with Apple silicon" and "on Apple Vision Pro". "Don't assume removing a supported destination in Xcode prevents distribution to that device type." `UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities` is "for genuine hardware dependencies, not to indicate distribution preferences." (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/test-compatible-devices, read 16 Nov 2026). G 2.4.1: "iPhone apps should run on iPad whenever possible." Mac and Vision Pro availability is a checkbox in App Store Connect › Pricing and Availability: "Your iPhone and iPad apps will be available to users on Apple Vision Pro unless you edit their availability" (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-your-apps-availability/manage-availability-of-iphone-and-ipad-apps-on-apple-vision-pro, read 16 Nov 2026); Macs likewise "provided no edits are made to the app availability" (…/manage-availability-of-iphone-and-ipad-apps-on-macs-with-apple-silicon, read 16 Nov 2026).
- **IPv6.** G 2.5.5: "Apps must be fully functional on IPv6-only networks." "Your app will be tested on a device running the latest released version of iOS." "If your app utilizes IPv4-specific APIs or hardcoded IP addresses, you'll need to make changes." Test on a Mac-shared NAT64 network (Apple's archived "Test for IPv6 DNS64/NAT64 Compatibility Regularly": Option-click Sharing, Internet Sharing, "Select the Create NAT64 Network checkbox", share over Wi-Fi) with "WWAN" off on the phone. (developer.apple.com/support/ipv6/, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Toolchain.** "Apps uploaded to App Store Connect must be built with Xcode 26 or later using an SDK for iOS 26, iPadOS 26, tvOS 26, visionOS 26, or watchOS 26." (since April 28, 2026). "iOS and iPadOS apps uploaded to App Store Connect must target iOS 13 or later." (since September 9, 2026) (developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Launch screen with the iOS 27 SDK.** "When you upload an app built with the iOS 27 SDK or later, App Store Connect validates that your app’s `Info.plist` contains at least one of the following keys": `UILaunchStoryboardName`, `UILaunchStoryboards`, `UILaunchScreen`, `UILaunchScreens`; otherwise "ITMS-90870: Missing launch screen." (developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3208-preparing-your-apps-launch-screen-to-meet-app-store-requirements, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Software.** G 2.5.1: "Apps may only use public APIs and must run on the currently shipping OS." G 2.5.2: no code that "introduces or changes features or functionality of the app". G 2.5.4: background services "only ... for their intended purposes". G 2.5.16: "Widgets, extensions, and notifications should be related to the content and functionality of your app."
- **Nothing hidden, nothing switched on later.** G 2.5.19: "Don’t include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features in your app; your app’s functionality should be clear to end users and App Review." G 2.3.1(a): "All new features, functionality, and product changes must be described with specificity in the Notes for Review section of App Store Connect (generic descriptions will be rejected)". Removal: apps may be removed when "The app includes hidden features or has been modified after approval, such as through a server-side change, in a way that introduces a violation." (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/safe-and-trusted-store/app-removals, read 16 Nov 2026). Apple on the new 2.5.19: it covers "adding features or functionality that weren’t included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review. Apps found engaging in this conduct are subject to removal from the App Store and removal from the Apple Developer Program." (developer.apple.com/news/?id=vvzx6cul, 16 Oct 2026, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Recording.** G 2.5.14: "Apps must request explicit user consent and provide a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording, logging, or otherwise making a record of user activity. This includes any use of the device camera, microphone, screen recordings, or other user inputs."
- **Extensions.** G 4.4: extensions "should include some functionality, such as help screens and settings interfaces where possible. You should clearly and accurately disclose what extensions are made available in the app’s marketing text, and the extensions may not include marketing, advertising, or in-app purchases."
- **Push.** G 4.5.4: "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function"; marketing pushes only if "customers have explicitly opted in ... via consent language displayed in your app’s UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out".
- **First-launch downloads.** G 4.2.3(ii): "If your app needs to download additional resources in order to function on initial launch, disclose the size of the download and prompt users before doing so."
- **Apple emoji.** G 4.5.6: "Apple emoji may not be used on other platforms or embedded directly in your app binary." G 5.2.5: "Apps and extensions ... may not include Apple emoji." (Unicode text that the system renders is allowed: "Apps may use Unicode characters that render as Apple emoji".)
- **Minimum functionality and spam.** G 4.2: "If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or “app-like,” it doesn’t belong on the App Store." G 4.2.6: "Apps created from a commercialized template or app generation service will be rejected unless they are submitted directly by the provider of the app’s content." G 4.3(b): "Don’t submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available."
- **Export compliance.** Without `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`, "App Store Connect walks you through an export compliance questionnaire every time you upload". "Typically, the use of encryption that’s built into the operating system—for example, when your app makes HTTPS connections using URLSession—is exempt from export documentation upload requirements, whereas the use of proprietary encryption is not." Exempt users "might alternatively be required to submit a year-end self-classification report to the U.S. government." (developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations, read 16 Nov 2026). Documentation needed: none for OS-provided encryption; "French encryption declaration" for a standard algorithm not provided by the OS (France only); CCATS plus the French declaration for proprietary algorithms (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/export-compliance-documentation-for-encryption, read 16 Nov 2026). A build in "Missing Compliance" can't be submitted until answered (…/manage-builds/choose-a-build-to-submit, read 16 Nov 2026).
- **Store quality.** "apps that crash on launch will be removed immediately." (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/safe-and-trusted-store/app-store-improvements, read 16 Nov 2026)

## 2. Purchases and subscriptions

**Digital goods use In-App Purchase.** G 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase." "you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." Outside the United States storefront, without an entitlement, "apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase" (G 3.1.1(a)); "These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps." Schedule 2 §3.11: "Subscription services purchased within Licensed Applications must use In-App Purchase."

**Subscription rules (G 3.1.2).**
- G 3.1.2(a): "you must provide ongoing value to the customer, and the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user’s devices." "Apps that attempt to scam users ... trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices ... will be removed".
- G 3.1.2(b): "Users should have a seamless upgrade/downgrade experience and should not be able to inadvertently subscribe to multiple variations of the same thing."
- G 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price. How many issues per month? How much cloud storage? What kind of access to your service? Ensure you clearly communicate the requirements described in Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement."

**Schedule 2 §3.8 exactly** (developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/, LYL262 dated October 29, 2026, read 16 Nov 2026):
- §3.8(a): "Auto-renew functionality must be on a weekly, monthly, bi-monthly, tri-monthly, semi-annual or annual basis".
- §3.8(b), the whole disclosure list: "You clearly and conspicuously disclose to users the following information regarding Your auto- renewing subscription:
  - Title of auto-renewing subscription, which may be the same as the in-app product name
  - Length of subscription
  - Price of subscription, and price per unit if appropriate
  Links to Your Privacy Policy and Terms of Use must be accessible within Your Licensed Application."
- §3.8(c): "You must fulfill the offer during the entire subscription period, as marketed, including any Billing Grace Period You authorize"; on breach Apple may refund "the full amount, or any portion thereof".
- §3.9: price increases for existing subscribers "in regions that require end user consent ... will be prompted to review and agree to the new price, otherwise the auto-renewal feature will be disabled."
- §3.10: "To the extent You promote and offer for sale auto-renewing subscriptions, within or outside of Your Licensed Application, You must do so in compliance with all legal and regulatory requirements."
- §4.2: your own EULA "must include and may not be inconsistent with the minimum terms and conditions specified on Exhibit D"; without one, use "shall be subject to Apple’s standard EULA". Exhibit D's ten headings: Acknowledgement, Scope of License, Maintenance and Support, Warranty, Product Claims, Intellectual Property Rights, Legal Compliance, Developer Name and Address, Third Party Terms of Agreement, Third Party Beneficiary.
- Note: the longer list that older templates paste (charged at confirmation, renews unless turned off 24 hours before, managed in account settings, unused trial forfeited) is not in this version. Auto-renewal and trial wording still comes from the subscriptions page, the HIG and auto-renewal laws (§3.10), below.

**The sign-up screen** (developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/, read 16 Nov 2026): "the following details must be included in your subscription’s sign-up screen:
- Subscription name and duration, and the content or services provided during the subscription period
- Full renewal price, shown clearly and prominently, and localized in available currencies
- A way for current subscribers to sign in or restore purchases"
"your app and App Store metadata must include links to your Terms of Use and Privacy Policy." "the amount that will be billed must be the most prominent pricing element in the layout"; a per-month breakdown or savings "should be displayed in a subordinate position and size to the annual price." "In the purchase flow for a free trial, clearly indicate how long the free trial lasts and the price billed once the free trial is over."

**HIG Apple In-App Purchase** (developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/in-app-purchase, read 16 Nov 2026):
- "If you offer an introductory price, be sure to list the introductory price, the duration of the offer, and the standard price the customer pays after the offer ends."
- "Clearly describe how a free trial works. It’s particularly important to make sure people know that when the free trial is over, a payment will be automatically initiated for the next subscription period."
- "Display the total billing price for each in-app purchase you offer, regardless of type."
- "Use the default confirmation sheet. ... Don’t modify or replicate this sheet."
- "Display your store only when people can make payments." (`AppStore.canMakePayments`)
- "Encourage a new subscription only when someone isn’t already a subscriber."
- "Always make it easy for customers to cancel an auto-renewable subscription. If the manage subscription action is deep within an app — or hard to recognize — subscribers can feel they’re being discouraged or prevented from canceling."
- Refunds: "Make it easy for people to request a refund. ... make sure this content doesn’t create a barrier to requesting a refund." "Avoid characterizing or providing guidance on Apple’s refund policies."

**Managing, cancelling, refunds.**
- `showManageSubscriptions(in:)` / SwiftUI `manageSubscriptionsSheet(isPresented:)` show "the same as what customers can view in their account settings"; on Mac only from Mac Catalyst / macOS 27.3; "For iOS apps running on Apple silicon, if isiOSAppOnMac is `true,` avoid showing the user interface for this feature." (developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/showmanagesubscriptions(in:), read 16 Nov 2026). Web link Apple gives: "https://apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions/" (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion, read 16 Nov 2026).
- Around the system sheet you may show an offer or a survey, but "always make it easy for people to access the system-provided management UI where they can cancel if they wish." (developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/, read 16 Nov 2026)
- Refunds in the app: `beginRefundRequest(in:)` / `refundRequestSheet(for:isPresented:onDismiss:)`; "The App Store takes up to 48 hours to either approve or deny a refund." (developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/beginrefundrequest(in:), read 16 Nov 2026). Web link Apple gives: "https://support.apple.com/118223".
- Schedule 2 §6.3: Apple may refund a customer who wants to cancel "within ninety (90) days of the date of download" (or the subscription period, if shorter).

**Getting purchases through review** (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/configure-in-app-purchases, read 16 Nov 2026):
- "App Review evaluates your In-App Purchases in the same sandbox environment you use for testing."
- "Paid In-App Purchases won't function in review until this agreement [the Paid Apps Agreement] is active."
- "Fetch product information using StoreKit APIs before presenting In-App Purchases in your app's UI to ensure the displayed details match your configuration in App Store Connect."
- "If you restrict In-App Purchases by storefront or device configuration, specify this in the Notes field".
- "Submit the In-App Purchases in App Store Connect once they're in the Ready for Review state."
- "Note: In-App Purchases do not need prior approval from App Review to function in review."
- First of each type: "The first consumable, non-consumable, auto-renewable subscription, and non-renewing subscription In-App Purchase of each type must be submitted with a new app version." "If you're submitting a new subscription it must be submitted together with its subscription group." "The app version, subscription group, and every subscription ... must all be added to the same draft submission before you click Submit for Review." (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-submissions-to-app-review/submit-an-in-app-purchase, read 16 Nov 2026)
- Required per subscription: Reference Name, Product ID, Availability, Subscription Duration, Subscription Price, Subscription Display Name, Description, **App Review Screenshot**; Review Notes and Image are optional; the group needs Subscription Levels, Reference Name, Display Name, App Name Display Options (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/required-localizable-and-editable-properties, read 16 Nov 2026).
- Products missing in the sandbox: bundle ID mismatch, In-App Purchase capability off, wildcard App ID, bad signing, inactive membership, "You don’t have a Paid Apps Agreement in effect", "incomplete or outdated banking or tax information"; edits "can take up to 1 hour" to reach the sandbox (developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3186-troubleshooting-in-app-purchases-availability-in-the-sandbox, read 16 Nov 2026).

**Other business rules.** G 2.3.2: the description, screenshots and previews "clearly indicate whether any featured items, levels, subscriptions, etc. require additional purchases." G 3.2.2(x): "Apps must not force users to rate the app, review the app, download other apps, or other store-related actions in order to access functionality". G 4.10: "You may not monetize built-in capabilities provided by the hardware or operating system". G 5.6: apps should never "trick them into making unwanted purchases, force them to share unnecessary data, raise prices in a tricky manner, charge for features or content that are not delivered".

## 4. Accounts and Sign in with Apple

**Deletion.** G 5.1.1(v): "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." "If your app doesn’t include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." "Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law." Social network connections: "The app must also include a mechanism to revoke social network credentials and disable data access between the app and social network from within the app. An app may not store credentials or tokens to social networks off of the device". G 5.1.1(x): name and email may be requested "so long as the request is optional for the user".

From developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion (the old URL developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/ redirects there), read 16 Nov 2026:
- "Make the account deletion option easy to find in your app. Typically, it's included in the app's account settings."
- "Offer to delete the entire account record, along with associated personal data. ... only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient."
- "If people need to visit a website to finish deleting their account, include a link directly to the page on your website where they can complete the process."
- Support flows: apps not in highly regulated industries "should not require people to make a phone call, send an email, use a chat support flow, or go through other support flows."
- Confirmation steps are fine, "However, apps that make it unnecessarily difficult for someone to delete their account will not pass review."
- Guest accounts: "People should have the option to delete automatically generated accounts (sometimes called "guest" accounts)".
- Slow deletion is fine: "Inform the person how long it will take to delete the account and provide a confirmation when the deletion is complete."
- Shared content: "This includes user-generated content that's shared with others, such as photos, videos, text posts, and reviews."
- Everywhere: "Everyone should be able to delete their accounts, regardless of where they're located."
- Subscriptions: "notify them that billing will continue through Apple and ask them to cancel their subscription before continuing." "you can offer to schedule account deletion for a later date, aligned with the subscription's expiration, as long as there's also an option to delete the account immediately."
- Signing up in a browser doesn't excuse it: "linking out to the default web browser to sign in or register an account provides a poor user experience and isn't appropriate".

HIG Managing accounts (read 16 Nov 2026): "don’t bury it in your Privacy Policy or Terms of Service pages"; "Provide a consistent account-deletion experience whether people perform it within your app or game or on the website"; "Even if people didn’t use your app to purchase the subscription, you still need to support account deletion."

**Sign in with Apple token revocation.**
- "Apps that support Sign in with Apple should use the Sign in with Apple REST API to revoke user tokens. Use server-to-server notification endpoints to process changes for Sign in with Apple accounts. Note that developers based in the Republic of Korea must provide a server‑to‑server notification endpoint when registering a new Services ID." (…/5-1-1-account-deletion, read 16 Nov 2026)
- The endpoint: `POST https://appleid.apple.com/auth/revoke`, form fields `client_id` ("must not include your Team ID"), `client_secret` (a JWT signed with the Sign in with Apple key), `token` (refresh or access token), `token_type_hint` (`refresh_token` or `access_token`); it "returns a `200` response code without a response body after the server invalidates the `token` value, or if the `token` value was previously invalidated." "In order to revoke authorization for a user, you must obtain a valid refresh token or access token." (developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapplerestapi/revoke-tokens, read 16 Nov 2026)
- TN3194 (first published 2025-10-03): "The Token revocation endpoint (`/auth/revoke`) is the only way to programmatically invalidate user tokens associated to your developer account without user interaction." At sign-up: "Verify the identity token and validate the authorization code using the `/auth/token` endpoint", store the token response, "Validate the refresh token up to once per day". Without a token: delete the data, "Direct the user to manually revoke access", respond to the revoked notification. If revocation is skipped, the person "won’t be presented with the initial authorization flow to enter their full name, email address, or both". On a revoke notification (app: `credentialRevokedNotification` plus `getCredentialState(forUserID:)`; server: `consent-revoked`), delete the token, server data and Keychain data, and "Revert the client to an unauthenticated state." (developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3194-handling-account-deletions-and-revoking-tokens-for-sign-in-with-apple, read 16 Nov 2026)
- Server notification event types: `email-disabled`, `email-enabled`, `consent-revoked`, `account-deleted`; the server needs TLS 1.2 or later (developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/processing-changes-for-sign-in-with-apple-accounts, read 16 Nov 2026).
- New relay domain: "new Sign in with Apple addresses, previously issued on privaterelay.appleid.com, will be issued on private.icloud.com"; apps "should ensure that their account systems, email validation logic, and allowlists accept addresses on the new private.icloud.com domain" (developer.apple.com/news/?id=1ptvdtcm, 24 Aug 2026, read 16 Nov 2026).

**Login services.** G 4.8: an app using "a third-party or social login service ... to set up or authenticate the user’s primary account with the app must also offer as an equivalent option another login service with the following features: the login service limits data collection to the user’s name and email address; the login service allows users to keep their email address private as part of setting up their account; and the login service does not collect interactions with your app for advertising purposes without consent." ## 5. Privacy: policy, permissions, consent, AI

- **Policy.** G 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." It must "Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data", confirm third parties "will provide the same or equal protection", and "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user’s data."
- **Consent, even for "anonymous" data.** G 5.1.1(ii): "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous at the time of or immediately following collection. Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data. Apps must also provide the customer with an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent."
- **Purpose strings.** G 5.1.1(ii): "Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data." Apple's help: "A purpose string that only restates the name of the resource, or promises a general benefit without saying what your app actually does, doesn't meet the requirement." "Write one brief, complete sentence in active voice and sentence case, ending with a period." "you must provide localized purpose strings." SDKs: "you're responsible for everything in your app ... If it doesn't [have a valid reason], remove references to those APIs from your app's binary." (developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-purpose-strings, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Pre-permission screens.** HIG Privacy (read 16 Nov 2026): "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert." "Use a term like “Continue” or “Next”"; "don’t provide a way for people to leave the screen or window without viewing the system alert — like offering an option to close or cancel." Before tracking: "offering incentives, displaying a screen or window that looks like a request, displaying an image of the alert, and annotating the screen behind the alert" cause rejection.
- **Minimise and respect.** G 5.1.1(iii): "Where possible, use the out-of-process picker or a share sheet rather than requesting full access to protected resources like Photos or Contacts." G 5.1.1(iv): "Apps must respect the user’s permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access." "Where possible, provide alternative solutions for users who don’t grant consent."
- **Sharing and third-party AI.** G 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." "You must receive explicit permission from users via the App Tracking Transparency APIs to track their activity." "Your app may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation".
- **Purpose limits.** G 5.1.2(ii): "Data collected for one purpose may not be repurposed without further consent". G 5.1.2(iii): "Apps should not attempt to surreptitiously build a user profile based on collected data".
- **AI-generated content is user-generated content.** G 1.2: "This includes social media, user-generated content platforms, and AI content creation apps." Such apps "must include: A method for filtering objectionable material ... A mechanism to report offensive content and timely responses to concerns ... The ability to block abusive users ... Published contact information". Chatbots offered as software in an app (G 4.7, 4.7.1) need the same filtering, reporting and blocking, and G 4.7.5 an age restriction "based on verified or declared age". Since 16 Oct 2026: "Content moderation requirements now explicitly cover nonconsensual intimate imagery — including imagery generated using AI tools — as content that apps must proactively prevent and remove from their platforms." (developer.apple.com/news/?id=vvzx6cul, read 16 Nov 2026)
- **Kids and location.** G 5.1.4 (children's privacy laws; "For Kids" terms reserved for the Kids Category); G 5.1.5: "Ensure that you notify and obtain consent before collecting, transmitting, or using location data."

## 7. App privacy labels

From developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/ (read 16 Nov 2026). The full data types and purposes: `ungenerated-audit-privacy/references/apple-privacy.md`.

- Required: "This information is required to submit new apps and app updates to the App Store." "You need to identify all of the data you or your third-party partners collect, unless the data meets all of the criteria for optional disclosure". Labels can change "at any time, and you do not need to submit an app update".
- "Collect": "“Collect” refers to transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." Data "processed only on device is not “collected”".
- App functionality counts: "even if you collect the data for reasons other than analytics or advertising, it still needs to be declared."
- Linked: "Data collected from an app is often linked to the user’s identity, unless specific privacy protections are put in place before collection"; "“Personal Information” and “Personal Data”, as defined under relevant privacy laws, are considered linked to the user."
- Tracking: "“Tracking” refers to linking data collected from your app about a particular end-user or device, such as a user ID, device ID, or profile, with Third-Party Data for targeted advertising or advertising measurement purposes, or sharing data collected from your app about a particular end-user or device with a data broker."
- Optional disclosure needs all four: not used for tracking; not used for third-party advertising, your advertising or other purposes; "Collection of the data occurs only in infrequent cases that are not part of your app’s primary functionality, and which are optional for the user"; provided in the interface with the person's name shown and chosen "each time". "data collected on an ongoing basis after an initial request for permission must be disclosed."
- Free text and voice: mark "Other User Content" and "Audio Data"; IP addresses are declared by use ("precise location, coarse location, device ID, or diagnostics"); web views: "Data collected via web traffic must be declared".
- Privacy links: "Privacy Policy (Required)", "Privacy Choices (Optional)".
- Publishing: App Store Connect › the app › App Privacy › Get Started, then Publish; the dialog confirms "your responses are accurate" (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy, read 16 Nov 2026).

## 11. After you submit

From developer.apple.com/help/app-review/ (pages under `after-submitting-for-review/` and `before-submitting-for-review/`) and G "After You Submit", read 16 Nov 2026.

- **What to put in App Review Information** (…/complete-review): "Incomplete submissions will be rejected." Fraud signals include "high-volume refund requests. (Guidelines 4.3 and 5.6.4)".
- **Reply, don't resubmit, when** App Review asks for information, or the fix is metadata or server-side ("such as providing or activating demo credentials"). "Resubmitting starts a new review, after which App Review can no longer reply to your previous message." Resubmit when the build changed. "Don’t resubmit until you’ve resolved every issue identified by App Review."
- **Talk to App Review.** "Reply to App Review in App Store Connect" is "the best way to receive assistance with an ongoing submission". A call: "you can use the Reply to App Review message window to request a call with an Apple representative", include your time zone; "Calls are typically completed within three to five business days." (…/contact-app-review)
- **Expedite.** "Only submissions that have already been sent to App Review can be expedited." For a critical bug, put it in the Notes; for an event, "include the event, its date, and how your app is associated with it". "Expedite requests should be reserved for matters of genuine urgency." (…/request-expedited-review; form at developer.apple.com/contact/app-store/?topic=expedite)
- **Appeal.** First "reply to the rejection ... and provide clarification"; then the App Review Board form; "Cite the specific guideline or terms your app was rejected or removed under, and explain point by point how your app meets it." "Only submit one appeal per rejected or removed app." Resubmitting closes the appeal. (…/appeal-to-the-app-review-board)
- **Bug Fix Submissions** (updates only): a rejected bug-fix update with no legal or safety issue can be approved if the message says "Bug Fix Submissions: The issues we've identified below are eligible to be resolved on your next update." Reply, don't resubmit.
- **Meet App Review before submitting.** "In each 30-minute video appointment, you can ask for advice on what to expect during review, how your app can best align with guidelines, reasons for common rejections". Request on the Meet with Apple page; "local business hours on Tuesdays and Thursdays"; include the app's Apple ID and specific questions. "A Meet with Apple appointment is informational. It doesn't replace, expedite, or guarantee the outcome" and App Review "cannot preapprove apps" (…/before-submitting-for-review/app-review-appointment).
- **Removal later.** Less severe issues "may be given 14 days to resolve"; safety or legal ones "may be immediately removed" (…/safe-and-trusted-store/app-removals).
