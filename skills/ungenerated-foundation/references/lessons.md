# What we learned, and why

The rules in this skill came from real rounds with one owner across several apps. Each lesson: the
rule, the reason (his words where he said it), and where it came from. Comma (a script-writing app),
Flamely (a focus tracker, phone and desktop), CutRoom (a video editor) and Pip are the apps. Their
exact looks are examples only; the rule is what transfers.

## The kit comes first

- **Make the kit before any screen: colours, type, buttons, press feel, grounds, motion.** "There
  should be a kit that is first being made that has button, animations, colours, everything." A friend
  who saw the board without a kit said the same thing first. (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Take the look from what the owner already has: their site, their brand, their desktop app.** Every
  invented palette was rejected; "look at my website, you will get a lot about me". A companion app
  traces every token to the product it accompanies. (CutRoom, Oct 2026; Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Copy the exact values of a look already picked; never approximate.** An invented peach gradient and
  plain dots replaced a chosen shade and pattern: "the colours are bad", "you didn't use the pattern
  that I chose". If a pick isn't recorded, say what you assumed. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Work the kit out back and forth, two or three options at a time, and lock each pick.** He chose by
  seeing, not by reading descriptions. (Comma, Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **A new app built on another app's engine gets its own look: reuse the engine, never the look.**
  Question screens made with a sibling app's board engine came out looking like that app and were
  rejected; the new app got its own progress bar, question layout and answer cards. "it looks
  identical to Flamely, I don't want a clone" (Comma, Oct 2026)

## Colour, grounds and surfaces

- **Never the default AI kit: indigo or violet, the trendy serif-plus-grotesk pair, generic
  gradients, sparkles.** "AI slop colours"; "the default AI pairings are the giveaway". It became a
  lint rule. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Grounds are flat and calm by default.** "Why is the gradient changing?"; "I don't like
  this grid background"; "I do not like the smoke particles"; "I don't like the shines". He once asked
  for a soft gradient, then banned gradients; the later rule wins: flat, with one or two named
  exceptions (the primary button's gel, one hero sky). (Flamely, Comma, Oct 2026)
- **If a ground changes at all, it changes never within a flow.** A gradient that shifted between
  screens read as a cut. (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Far fewer shadows; none behind icons, logos or emoji.** "Overused shadows make things look less
  polished and less minimal"; "every icon has a shadow, which I do not like". Use hairlines and a soft
  inner glow instead. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Premium is soft borderless surfaces on a warm or neutral ground.** No outlines, no hard gloss, no
  paper textures, tape or handwriting fonts: the paper-and-tape round was rejected: he wanted "an app feel".
  (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Prefer independent elements to boxed cards, and never double-outline.** "I do not like the boxed
  look ... I like independent look." (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Don't overuse any one colour.** "There is nothing wrong with brown, but AI uses brown excessively."
  Overuse is the tell, not the colour. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **One signature element, in one place.** A paper script sheet was loved; paper everywhere was hated.
  "If everything is unique, nothing in the app is." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **A slow-drifting pattern can carry identity, if AI tools don't default to it.** Swirling contour
  lines were his; dots and pluses are everyone's. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **The selected state never reuses a colour that already means something.** Green and yellow already
  meant two kinds of text, so a highlighter selection confused them. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Follow the phone's light or dark setting unless the owner sets a default.** Design both by hand.
  (Flamely, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Build the ground as one layer: sky, grain, pattern and any filter or fade together, and check it
  in every state.** A sky band that ended in a hard line, a pattern that started only below the sky,
  and a grey filter that reached some layers and not others each left a visible seam. "these little
  things really matter to me" (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Borrow a game's loop, not its toy look.** Gamification stays subtle (a path, stamps, ticks, a
  streak): the owner asked for "more gamified, like Duolingo", then rejected a round of chunky 3D
  pressable buttons and tiles: "moving into gamey territory, doesn't feel good". (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **What opens over the app blurs what's behind it; never a flat dim.** A menu opening over the home
  screen on a plain dark scrim was asked to blur the content instead. "I do want like the things to be
  more blurry" (Comma, Nov 2026)

## Type

- **Pair one expressive headline face (with one accent word in italic) with a neutral sans.** "This
  font looks very aesthetically pleasing; it looks unique." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Plain, thick, readable type; no novelty display faces.** "I'm not a big fan of weird-looking fonts
  ... I like thick text." (CutRoom, Oct 2026)
- **Check custom fonts at the weights you use before showing anything.** Some rendered "wonky", and
  variable fonts defaulted to Light until the weight axis was set. (Flamely, Pip, Oct 2026)
- **No small text.** "No one is gonna read that, so you have to be very clear with the headings."
  (Flamely, Oct–Nov 2026)

## Shape

- **Corner radii stay consistent across nested shapes: inner = outer − padding.** "The rounded edges of
  the calendar doesn't look good at all." (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Radius is a pick, not a default.** One owner liked soft rounded cards in a phone app and wanted
  near-square corners in a pro desktop tool: "too much rounded radius". Ask. (Comma, CutRoom, Oct 2026)
- **Selected tiles never touch.** Keep a gap and draw the ring inside the tile, or picks merge into one
  blob. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Words never leak out of their shape.** A label crossing a line or pattern sits on a small pill.
  (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **A group of options wraps into even rows of equal-width options, never a ragged last row, and
  anything in columns lines up exactly.** A second row of uneven choices and a calendar's weekday
  letters sitting off their day columns were both sent back as alignment bugs. (Comma, Oct 2026)

## One job per screen

- **Each screen does one job and reads at a glance; cut everything else.** "The app is way too complex
  to even use. I made the app and I'm not able to understand it." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Compact by default.** Two related cards become two tabs of one card; secondary text collapses.
  "Compacting is the rule of the UX." He disliked expanding sections and preferred tabs. (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Far less text, more space; the chrome around the person's words is almost wordless.** "All of
  this is extremely text-heavy"; "remove little sub text please". (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **The first screens hold one idea each.** "There's like five logos in the onboarding." (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **One way to do each thing, one look per control.** Two add buttons were slop; one mic inside every
  text box, "so things are consistent". (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Use the platform's own navigation.** A text-only tab bar was "horrendous"; a custom bar of
  unlabelled glyphs was liked once, then reversed: "the previous navigation bar was pretty, glossy, how
  native apps do". Native wins. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Don't dress the app up as something it isn't.** A weekday strip with no way to see other days was
  "a calendar app"; the job was a list of ideas. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Home shows what to act on now,** not everything ever saved. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **An empty screen centres one useful action, never two.** "Anything that is empty, we have to give
  them action." (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Never type into a system alert inside a flow; type in place.** (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Ship the smallest version that does the core job.** "Decrease the bloat as much as possible."
  When a scope fails, cut back to what works. (CutRoom, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Give each feature one meaning, and never blend two features into one screen or step.** A request
  to add a video to the library (something you wait for) once also asked for the person's own take on
  it (something instant); the owner stopped the round to pull them apart. "the library feature is
  totally separate from angle feature" (Comma, Oct 2026)

## State and icons

- **A state is an icon, never a coloured row or a fraction.** "I'm not liking the blue and the weird
  orange stuff ... we should be representing that by icon"; "four of 15 beats? Why does it even show
  that?" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Each kind of object gets one container style, used everywhere.** "We need to have the container
  for scripts, containers for films ... so I don't have to second-guess things." (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Real apps get their real icon; things that aren't apps get an emoji or illustration, never a drawn
  tile.** "Google Docs is not an option here, it's not an icon." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **The whole identity mark is the hero, with nothing glowing behind it.** A bare flame was "just not
  our identity"; the full candle was. (Flamely, Oct 2026)

## Inputs

- **Where an input can be a toy, make it one:** a ruler under a needle, a bar chart you drag, a calendar
  that fills, a swipe deck. "I really love this age slider, wow." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Explain anything abstract with a tiny screen that plays it,** not an icon or a list. (Comma, Oct 2026)

## Words and numbers

- **Name every feature in words a newcomer understands.** "I don't know what draft leak is"; "Add to
  your notes. What do you mean by that?" (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Never label the person with a persona or category.** "Generalizing things that don't exist is what
  AI does." Talk about their time and their work. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Say the promise plainly; no corny lines.** "Stop posting into the void" was corny. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Write like a person; if a line could be in any AI-made app, rewrite it.** (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Copy says something concrete; if the reader learns nothing, rewrite it.** "These scripts tell us
  nothing." (CutRoom, Nov 2026)
- **Frame the core action so nobody feels guilty using it.** "You are only borrowing their shape, not
  copying them." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Show only numbers the app can measure and that help a decision.** Exaggerate the scale, keep the
  maths honest; the person's own numbers beat template stats. (Flamely, Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **No demo data. Run on a real day one and live content.** "The demo data is ruining the quality of
  everything." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Give each product word one meaning, and lint the copy for its wrong use.** "Shape" already meant a
  story's beats, so calling formats "shapes" confused the owner, and genre labels (POV, skit,
  listicle) described formats wrongly; a format became how a video is made (face or not, voice or not,
  what the footage is, how many people). "I don't know what you mean by shapes" (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Name an option by what the person gets, not by how the app makes it.** A choice labelled with its
  mechanism (the AI writes three drafts) confused the owner; it became the kind of video they want,
  and the several takes appear after. (Comma, Nov 2026)

## Research

- **Study the best apps in the category before designing a flow, and say that you did.** "Are you even
  looking at Brainrot? Are you using the Appllama MCP first?" (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Take intent and mechanics, never the look.** "Why are you becoming a copycat? Understand the
  intention." (Flamely, Oct 2026; Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Study references in depth and measure them.** Skimming a reference set the owner gave was called a
  failure; breaking reels down frame by frame gave hard numbers. (CutRoom, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Put yours beside theirs.** The first redesign he liked came after laying his screen next to the
  category leader's. (CutRoom, Oct 2026)

## Naming

- **Pick a short one-word or character name, check the domain, and confirm before any rename.** He
  changed his mind on a rename within the hour. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Before anyone uses a name, the mascot's included, check the App Store in several countries, the
  trademark register (software classes 9 and 42) and the domains, and keep platform marks out of it.**
  The working name had a live trademark application for mobile software and two products in its niche,
  two mascot candidates were a rival AI teleprompter and a video app's registered mark, and "Reels" is
  Meta's registered mark. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **If the person does the work, name their craft; a name ending in "-ify" or carrying "AI" says the
  machine does it.** Both "-ify" names in the niche generated scripts with AI, and of 137 script apps
  51 pitched AI (27 in the name), so the name moved toward the person writing (ink, draft, take).
  (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Keep every word the app shows in one copy file, so a rename is one edit, and lint for the old name
  with any whitespace between its words.** A rename by hand touched about 70 files, and leftovers of
  the old name still showed later, one in a title split over two lines. (Comma, Oct 2026)

## The app's website

- **A phone only where it shows something nothing else can.** A phone in every section was called "tons
  and tons of phone is never what I meant". Sections that explain themselves (a card, a playing reel, a
  before and after) get none. (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **An integration is one section, never the page's story.** When the app also works inside an AI
  assistant (a connector, a plugin), give it one section late in the page, its phone playing the chat as
  it happens (the command typed, the tool working, the results landing in the app). Spread over several
  steps, or in the hero, it made the app look like "an extension of ChatGPT or Claude, which is not what it
  is". Name only what really works in each assistant (a slash command in one, picking the app from a
  menu in another). (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Rebuild from the round the owner liked.** After a rejected round, start again from the last version
  they praised and add only what they asked for. (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Lay the website out zoomed out and calm, with a hero small enough to fit a laptop's first
  screen.** Phones and type at a calm size with room round them, like a premium product page; a page
  blown up to fill the screen was rejected, and the hero's headline, lede, phone and fields shrank
  until it showed whole without scrolling. "this landing page is too zoomed in" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Give the website a navigation bar (logo, sections, the main call to action) and a hero that shows
  the promise working in real motion.** A headline beside a still phone, with no navigation, didn't
  read as a real app's page. "there's no navigation, so add navigation bar, obviously"
  (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Sections show the real app, never explanatory widgets, mock cards or framed boxes.** Sections
  built as diagrams inside frames were rejected as not a landing page. "the showcasing doesn't look
  good at all" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Build the website from the app's approved pieces, and make it move at least as well as the
  onboarding.** The screenshots' type and colours, real content looping as video, the character and
  the onboarding's own animations: a first site that moved less than the app's onboarding was called
  worse than it. "The reels should like looped out" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **A before-to-after transformation plays by itself when it comes into view; the reader never scrubs
  it with the scroll.** The idea-to-script section first moved only as you scrolled and was asked to
  play on its own. (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **A website demo never asks for a device permission; it plays by itself and still answers drags.**
  The voice-following demo was rebuilt to play the wave and type each sentence on its own, and the
  privacy policy says the site never asks for the microphone. "I do not want them to kind of like ask
  for their permission for Mike" (Comma, Nov 2026)

## Images

- **Size images for the largest place they show, in device pixels.** A 320 px frame in a half-width
  tile on a 3× iPhone is stretched 1.7× (3.7× across the screen): fine as a thumbnail, visibly soft in
  a tile meant to show the content, so the tiles got a 720 px set. (Comma, Oct 2026)

## Concept and planning

- **Help with the step before the core one.** A writing app rests on idea, script and film, but people
  arrive without an idea; with only catching and keeping ideas built, helping people find them (their
  own story, a topic, others' work) became work of its own. "ideas ... from their own brain, copying
  others, Reddit" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Before "connect your account" goes in the plan, check what each platform's API can read, and offer
  it only where it can.** Instagram's API reads only Business and Creator accounts (personal accounts
  have had none since the Basic Display API ended in December 2024) after Meta's app review, and
  TikTok and YouTube each need their own review; the connect step left the onboarding, and results are
  logged by hand until a real connection exists. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Before promising a turnaround that a person or a pipeline delivers, do the capacity maths against
  the real pace.** The manual process had made 5 breakdowns in 28 days (about 10 a week at best) while
  requests at 1,000 users would need about 49 a week, so processing had to be automated before launch;
  for weeks the app promised an answer "within 72 hours" with nothing processing the queue. "they
  request, I process it." (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Do the riskiest unknowns first, each as a short spike with a named fallback, and finish the
  list.** Direct push to Apple was ranked first (an hour to test, a push service as the fallback) and
  shipped first try; Sign in with Apple token revocation, fourth on the list, was left for later and
  was still missing at the pre-submission audit. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Keep one diagram-led architecture document in the repo, with a numbered decisions page that
  changes first.** Each decision has its status, who made it, the date and the chapter it changes, all
  in the same commit; open questions carry a yes-or-no recommendation and replaced decisions are
  struck through, never deleted, so a later session on another machine builds without the chat. "this
  is where we will make changes, we will discuss ideas" (Comma, Oct 2026)

## Working with the owner

- **Follow a plan or wireframes with styled screens quickly; a plan alone reads as no design.** A
  board of lo-fi wireframes got one answer the same day. "where is the design?" (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Ask for the why behind the owner's decisions in one numbered list, and log each answer against its
  rule.** He answers by voice, down the list, without saying the numbers (and his dictation tool
  numbers sentences on its own), so match answers by topic; a rule that knows its reason is applied
  rightly to cases it never named. "all the little decisions I made, you can ask me why"
  (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **"It depends" is an answer: log the rule as a default, not a never.** Logged as a hard rule, it
  makes the next round refuse cases the owner would have allowed. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Read swearing as emphasis about the work, not anger at you.** Answer with what is approved, what
  is left and the one next step, then fix it; no apology and no long explanation. "What do I do now?
  Should I quit? Tell me." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Never guess the end of an unfinished sentence.** Dictated messages often stop mid-thought and the
  next message usually finishes it: wait for it, then ask, quoting the fragment. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Keep the owner's taste as a keyed log that generates the rules file, praise included.** Every note
  gets a stable key, so a newer note replaces the older rule and repeats are counted (938 notes became
  751 rules in force); the rules, their lint checks and a list of golden references (what he loved,
  studied before designing) are rebuilt from the log each round, never edited by hand. "improve these
  skills and automated learning mechanism for these AI slop stuff" (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **When a choice can't be put into words, hand the owner the exported screens to edit in his own
  tool, then build from his edit.** His edited picture carries what he can't describe, one step at a
  time, back and forth. "so I can edit them in the Photoshop ... and give it back to you"
  (Comma, Nov 2026)

- **The owner's character is the only character.** When the owner has drawn or rigged one, use it everywhere and use it a
  lot; never generate a stand-in, and once replaced the old one never comes back. "Use the character that I made ...
  never use this ever again." (Pip, Nov 2026)
- **3D props share the character's art style.** Books, shelves and objects rendered in 3D still take the character's
  palette, soft shading and rounded shapes, across the whole app. "Can we make the books match the art style of the
  character even if they are 3D?" (Pip, Nov 2026)
- **Icons get redrawn in the character's style, from a list.** Keep every icon and picture on the board with the screens
  it appears on, plus one style prompt to paste before each subject. (Pip, Nov 2026)
- **Start from what's in the folder.** A design board already in the project is the design tool: update it to match the
  app, never rebuild it from scratch. "This board was already in the folder, why didn't you do that?" (Pip, Nov 2026)
- **Write his decisions down the round he makes them, small ones too.** "Add all the knowledge and micro decisions I'm
  making, saving these lessons": the name he uses for a place, a colour said in passing, what he loves, what he waves
  away. The project's memory gets each with his words and the date; the skill gets the ones another app would meet. Ask
  nothing twice. (Pip, 27 Nov 2026)
- **He dictates: read for intent, keep his words.** Notes arrive spoken, with slips and no punctuation. Work out what he
  means, then keep the exact words beside your reading wherever the note travels (a brief, the memory, the report), so a
  wrong reading can be caught. (Pip, 27 Nov 2026)
- **"Show me" means pictures of the real thing, now.** Mid-build, send a labelled sheet of real screenshots of the
  screens that are true, and name the ones that are not ready and why. Never a description, never a screen whose picture
  missed passed off as done. (Pip, 27 Nov 2026)
- **Keep building while he reviews.** New notes on the board arrive while the app is being built: the board round and the
  build run side by side, and a screen whose design just changed is ported again afterwards, said plainly. (Pip, 27 Nov
  2026)
- **A plan line the owner can't follow has failed.** Say what changes for a person, with an example, never server words
  (logs, bot checks, sync, versions). Two technical lines came back "didn't get it" and "I don't understand". (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Every limit gets its scale answer.** When a number comes up (a cap, a quota, a plan), say what happens at ten times
  today's users and what raising it takes. "What if we do have like 5k users at some point?" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **A cookbook for each command the owner will use.** What to type, what happens behind it step by step, what comes back
  and where it lands, what it costs and whose usage it spends. "Give me cook book for each so I understand and know what
  is happening" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **A plan page shows only what's left.** In this order: what waits for the owner's word, what you're unsure about,
  what isn't done (marked plainly), and a short Done list at the bottom saying what to try in the newest build. Finished
  items leave the working list; before writing "being fixed", check the code. "This document is way too much to go
  through" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Name a screen by the path to it.** In anything the owner reads, write the taps that reach it (the tab, the button,
  the page), never your own nickname for the screen. "Which screen are we talking about here?" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **A new test build goes to the owner first.** Keep testers in a group that doesn't receive builds automatically, on
  the last build that was theirs; a new build reaches them only when the owner says. "For now not for everybody, for
  now only for me" (Comma, Nov 2026)
